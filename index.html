Filament-Spool Re-wind Machine

Design Engineering Project 202 - ATU Sligo

Project Objectives

This project focuses on the development of a Filament-Spool Re-wind Machine, transitioning the design into a highly functional, safe, and sustainable product. Our primary focus is on mechanical reliability, user inclusivity, and eco-friendly manufacturing.

  • Automation & Safety: Integrated sensors and a mechanical limit switch to ensure the spool is properly seated before the motor can operate.
  • Inclusivity (DEI): Tool-less spool changing system and clear color-coded controls to accommodate users of all experience levels and physical abilities.
  • Ergonomic Table: An adjustable height workbench to suit different users, complete with secure storage for 4 full and 4 empty filament spools.

Engineering Methodology

Sustainability (Goal 12)

We optimized our 3D printed parts to reduce filament use and utilized laser-cutting for flat components (gears/connecting rods) to reduce material waste and energy consumption. We selected recyclable thermoplastics like PLA and PETG.

DFMA & Manufacturability

The design uses bolts instead of adhesives, allowing for easy disassembly, maintenance, and part replacement. Bearings and springs can be replaced individually without discarding the whole system.

Safety Integration

A physical safety guard shield was designed to cover the top gears. An infrared sensor monitors the filament path, and automation prevents snapping or overloading without human supervision.

Technical Development

Primary Drive System

The reciprocating mechanism is powered by an RS PRO 12V DC brushed geared motor, outputting 79 rpm with a maximum torque of 6000 g.cm. A custom Nema 23 Stepper motor is utilized for the table mechanism.

Control & Feedback

We developed custom Arduino software and circuitry to control the motor. The board includes an enable push button, status indicator LEDs, a potentiometer for speed control, an ultrasonic sensor, and a MOSFET/diode setup for motor safety.

Virtual Testing & FEA Simulation

To ensure structural integrity before manufacturing, we conducted comprehensive Finite Element Analysis (FEA) using ANSYS. We performed a static structural analysis on the core components, including the custom segmented gear/rail and the cantilever shaft.

Our simulations compared PLA against Structural Steel for a 50 mm cantilever shaft assembly subjected to a 700 N load. The results allowed us to iterate the design, proving a max Von Mises stress of 7.15 MPa and validating our hand calculations.

Prototyping & Media

Physical Mechanism

Combining 3D printed components with laser-cut plates (PMMA/Acrylic) significantly improved production time and accuracy while reducing material waste.

Tension Control

A dedicated filament tension control mechanism utilizes Igus bearings (BB-604-B180-30-ES) and custom 3D printed springs for optimal performance.

ATU Makerspace

Iterative testing and fabrication were heavily supported by the ATU Makerspace equipment, allowing us to test and fix gear alignment issues swiftly.
